What was observed (self-review, oss:auditor pass on #58's own diff): `boot()`'s own
handshake read -- `$handshake = $this->readFrame($parentSocket);`, in RectorRunner::boot() --
is called with no `$deadlineNs` argument, so it keeps the pre-#58 behaviour of looping on a
socket timeout forever. #58's --call-timeout was scoped, by the maintainer's own design
comment on the issue, to "the fork-path wait loop and the persistent worker<->daemon read"
(forkAndExecute() and runForked()) -- boot()'s handshake was never named as in scope, so this
may be a deliberate line rather than an oversight.

Where: src/RectorRunner.php, boot() (the fork+handshake sequence that starts a fresh worker),
as distinct from forkAndExecute()/runForked() (the two loops #58 actually covers).

What it would cost a caller: a worker that wedges during its OWN container build --
before it ever answers boot()'s handshake -- blocks the very first run() call indefinitely,
with --call-timeout doing nothing, even though the feature's stated purpose is "a genuinely
wedged process is killed and reaped instead of blocking the caller forever." This is a
narrower window than a wedged CALL (boot happens once per worker, not once per call), but the
same class of unbounded wait #58 exists to close.

How it was confirmed: read directly (not run) -- forkAndExecute()'s and runForked()'s own
deadline plumbing was compared against boot()'s call to readFrame(), which passes no
$deadlineNs and therefore takes the null default (loop-on-timeout-forever, unchanged since
before #58).

Possibly a real gap, possibly intentionally out of #58's stated scope -- the maintainer is the
one who can settle it in one sentence: does #58's acceptance criterion cover a wedge during
BOOT, or only a wedge during an established call? If the former, the fix is the same shape as
runForked()'s own deadline (compute callDeadlineNs(), pass it to readFrame() in boot(), and on
RectorCallTimeoutException there SIGKILL+reap the half-booted worker pid and throw a clean
error) -- not implemented here because it was not named as in scope and changing it changes
the blast radius of what #58 touches.
